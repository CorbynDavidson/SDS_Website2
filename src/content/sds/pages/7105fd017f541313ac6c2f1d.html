What to Do When Your Landlord Keeps Delaying

30 | 01 | 26

By SheldonDavidsonSolicitors

Landlords have a legal duty to keep your home in a safe, liveable condition. But for many tenants, getting essential repairs carried out is a constant battle. Problems are reported, then ignored. Appointments are made, then missed. Weeks pass with no updates, while the disrepair worsens and the landlord remains silent.

Whether you're living with damp and mould, broken heating, faulty electrics or structural damage, delays like this are more than just frustrating, they are unacceptable. You pay rent for a home that meets basic legal standards. When those standards aren’t met, and your landlord fails to act within a reasonable time, you have the right to challenge them.

At SDS Solicitors, we help tenants across England take back control. If your landlord keeps making excuses, delays repairs, or ignores complaints entirely, we can help you demand urgent action, enforce your legal rights, and secure the compensation you deserve.

When Delay Becomes Neglect

Some repairs take time to arrange, and not every delay is deliberate. But when serious problems are left unresolved, despite repeated complaints and mounting evidence, your landlord’s failure to act becomes more than poor service. It becomes neglect.

Delays are especially serious when they involve:

  • Leaks causing water damage or damp
  • Faulty electrics or broken heating systems
  • Structural defects, including flooring, ceilings or staircases
  • Health hazards such as mould, pest infestations or sewage issues

If your landlord has been made aware of the issue and still fails to take action within a reasonable timeframe, they may be in breach of the Landlord and Tenant Act 1985 and other housing laws. You are not expected to wait indefinitely, or live in unsafe, deteriorating conditions.

Many landlords rely on tenant inaction. They assume you won’t escalate. But long delays are not something you have to accept. When your health, safety, or quality of life is at risk, legal action is not just an option, it may be the only effective solution.

Why Landlords Stall Repairs

When repairs are delayed again and again, it’s easy to feel ignored or worse, blamed. But in many cases, these delays are not accidental. Landlords, especially larger housing providers, may postpone repairs strategically, hoping tenants will give up, fix the issue themselves, or stop pushing altogether. Common tactics include:

  • Blaming contractors or “approval delays”
  • Promising call-backs that never happen
  • Sending out inspectors but never following up
  • Offering temporary fixes that don’t resolve the issue
  • Shifting responsibility from one department to another

These delay tactics wear tenants down. Some feel pressured to stay quiet for fear of being labelled a “problem tenant.” Others are made to feel that the disrepair is somehow their fault.

The result? Weeks, months, or even years of living in unsafe conditions, while the landlord avoids accountability.

But the law is clear: landlords are responsible for ensuring their properties remain in a good state of repair. And if they don’t, tenants have the right to take legal action, backed by evidence, expert support, and a strong legal team behind them.

What Can You Do When Repairs Keep Being Delayed?

If your landlord keeps delaying essential repairs, there are practical steps you can take to protect yourself and strengthen your case:

  • Document the disrepair – Take clear, dated photographs or videos of the issue and monitor any changes or worsening conditions over time.
  • Report everything in writing – Always report the issue via email or letter. Avoid relying on verbal conversations. Written proof is key if the matter escalates.
  • Keep a diary – Note how the disrepair is affecting your daily life. Are you unable to use part of your home? Has your health worsened? Are your heating bills higher due to broken windows or damp?
  • Chase the landlord – If there’s no response within a reasonable timeframe, follow up. Be firm, and state clearly that the matter is urgent.
  • Contact your local council – If your landlord continues to ignore you, the council’s Environmental Health team can inspect the property and issue enforcement notices.
  • Seek legal advice – If none of the above steps work, don’t wait. Legal support can compel your landlord to act and may entitle you to compensation.

Delayed repairs are not just frustrating—they’re unacceptable. You have rights, and you do not need to face the situation alone.

Seek Legal Advice Early

If repairs continue to be delayed despite your efforts, it’s vital to act quickly and strategically. The sooner you take control of the situation, the more likely you are to achieve a positive outcome.
