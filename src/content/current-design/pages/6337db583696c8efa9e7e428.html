Understanding Insect Infestations in Housing Disrepair

Insect infestations can arise from underlying disrepair issues that make your home vulnerable to pests.

Defective roofing, for example, can create entry points for insects, while persistent moisture and conditions that may also support a Damp & Mould Claim can attract certain pests and worsen the overall condition of the property.

Common insects in housing disrepair cases include ants, cockroaches, flies, and wood-destroying insects.

Under UK housing law, landlords are responsible for addressing the disrepair that facilitates infestations. The Landlord and Tenant Act 1985 requires landlords to maintain the structure and exterior of the property, including repairing defects that allow insects to enter.

Additionally, the Homes (Fitness for Human Habitation) Act 2018 obligates landlords to ensure their properties are free from hazards that could endanger tenants’ health or well-being, including pest-related issues stemming from disrepair.

If your landlord has been informed of disrepair issues contributing to an insect infestation but has failed to act, they may be in breach of their legal obligations.

This could make you eligible to pursue an insect infestation claim.

Inpact of Insect Infestations on Your Home and Well-being

Insect infestations linked to disrepair can have a profound impact on your daily life. Infestations often cause damage to your property, such as chewing through wood, damaging furniture, or contaminating food supplies. The presence of pests can also exacerbate existing disrepair, such as worsening damp or decay.

The health risks posed by infestations are equally concerning, particularly where the problem extends beyond insects and forms part of wider Pest & Vermin Infestation  Claims involving rodents or other pests.

Cockroaches and flies, for instance, can spread harmful bacteria and increase the likelihood of food contamination, while insect bites or allergens may trigger skin irritations or respiratory issues. Beyond the physical impacts, infestations can cause significant emotional distress, leaving tenants feeling unsafe or uncomfortable in their own homes.

How to Make a Housing Disrepair Claim

If you are considering a housing disrepair claim, you will usually need to demonstrate that your landlord was made aware of the problem but failed to put things right within a reasonable period.

This may involve showing that:

  1. You reported the disrepair to your landlord: You should be able to demonstrate that your landlord or housing provider was told about the roofing or guttering problem. Emails, letters, text messages and repair reports can all provide useful evidence.

  2. Your landlord did not carry out repairs within a reasonable timeframe: Once your landlord has been notified, they should be given a reasonable opportunity to investigate and complete the necessary repairs. If the issue is ignored or allowed to continue unnecessarily, you may have grounds to pursue a claim.

  3. The disrepair has affected you or caused financial loss: You may be entitled to compensation where the problem has damaged your possessions, affected your health or prevented you from properly enjoying your home. This can include having to live with damp, leaks, unsafe conditions or ongoing disruption.

Compensation for Insect Infestations in Housing Disrepair

When a landlord fails to address disrepair that leads to an insect infestation, tenants may be entitled to compensation. The amount of compensation depends on the severity of the infestation, the duration of the issue, and the extent of the inconvenience caused.

Compensation typically accounts for the loss of enjoyment of your home and any stress or disruption caused by the infestation. It can also factor in financial losses, such as the cost of replacing damaged property or seeking temporary pest control measures.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I prove the infestation is caused by disrepair?

You can document the connection between the infestation and the disrepair with photos, videos, and any reports from pest control or property inspectors. Our team can help you gather and present the necessary evidence.

What if my landlord blames me for the infestation?

If the infestation is clearly linked to structural disrepair, such as defective roofing or damp, your landlord is legally responsible for resolving the issue. We can help you demonstrate that the infestation is not due to your actions.

Can I include the cost of pest control in my claim?

Yes, if you have incurred expenses for pest control due to your landlord’s failure to act, these costs can be included as part of your insect infestation claim.
