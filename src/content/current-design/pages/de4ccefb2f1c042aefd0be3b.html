Understanding Defective Windows and Doors in Housing Disrepair

Windows and doors play a crucial role in keeping your home safe, secure, and protected from the elements. Legally, landlords are responsible for maintaining these structural elements to ensure that tenants are not exposed to unsafe or uncomfortable living conditions. When windows and doors are faulty, they can create a range of issues that impact both your health and security.

Draughts from ill-fitting or broken windows and doors can make it difficult to keep your home warm, leading to increased heating costs and potential health problems caused by cold and damp conditions. Water ingress from gaps or damaged seals can result in damp patches and mould growth, which can contribute to respiratory issues and allergies. Faulty locks or broken frames also pose a security risk, making it easier for intruders to gain access to your home and leaving you feeling unsafe.

If you have reported these problems to your landlord and they have failed to take action, this could be a breach of their legal obligations. If you’re looking to make a window or door disrepair claim, get in touch.

Types of Disrepair Issues That Cause Problems with Windows and Doors

Broken or Poorly Sealed Windows

Windows that are cracked, broken, or suffering from faulty seals often lead to damp, heat loss, and ongoing discomfort for tenants. When these issues are left unaddressed, they can also cause the surrounding frames to deteriorate, potentially giving rise to rotten window frame compensation if the landlord fails to act. Tenants experiencing persistent drafts, water ingress, or structural decay may be entitled to pursue damaged window claims, defective window claims, or faulty window claims, depending on the nature of the problem and the landlord’s response. These claims help ensure essential repairs are completed and recognise the inconvenience and impact caused by long term disrepair.

Loose or Damaged Door Frames

Doors with damaged or loose frames can allow draughts to enter, impacting the energy efficiency of the property and potentially compromising security if the door fails to close or lock properly. They can also go rotten which could entitle you to rotten door compensation.

Faulty Locks and Hinges

Windows and doors with broken locks or hinges can create a security risk, leaving your home vulnerable to intruders. Faulty locks may also make it difficult to safely exit in the event of an emergency.

Condensation and Mould from Poor Ventilation

Inadequate ventilation from defective windows and doors can lead to condensation build-up, which can encourage mould growth. This is especially concerning in properties with poor airflow, as damp conditions can exacerbate respiratory problems for occupants.

Poor Insulation Leading to Increased Heating Costs

Draughty or improperly insulated windows and doors allow heat to escape, increasing heating bills and making it harder to keep rooms warm. This can lead to higher energy costs and increased discomfort for tenants.

Compensation for Defective Windows and Doors in Housing Disrepair

If your landlord has failed to address issues with your windows or doors, you may be entitled to compensation. Compensation amounts are typically based on factors such as the severity of the defect, how long the issue has persisted, and any inconvenience or property damage caused as a result. Compensation in housing disrepair cases generally ranges between 25% and 50% of the rent paid during the period of disrepair. Our experienced solicitors will assess your case and determine the level of compensation you may be entitled to.

Filing a housing disrepair claim can not only motivate landlords to carry out necessary repairs, but also provide financial relief for any discomfort, inconvenience, or health effects that defective windows and doors have caused.

If your landlord is not addressing your complaints, document the disrepair with photos and written notices, and keep records of all correspondence. Then, contact a housing disrepair solicitor who can help you enforce your landlord’s responsibilities.

Are landlords responsible for broken windows?

Yes. In most rented homes, including council and housing association properties, landlords are legally responsible for ensuring that windows are kept in safe, secure, and functional condition. This includes repairing broken panes, damaged frames, faulty locks, and any defects that affect the property’s structure, security, or ability to keep out the elements.

If a window becomes broken through no fault of the tenant and the landlord fails to put it right within a reasonable timeframe, this may amount to a breach of their repairing duties. Broken windows can expose occupants to cold, damp, and security risks, so landlords are expected to act promptly once the issue has been reported.

Tenants should always notify their landlord in writing and keep clear records of the damage. If the problem is ignored or long standing, specialist legal support may be needed to ensure repairs are completed and that the tenant is properly supported throughout the broken window compensation claim process.

SDS Solicitors can advise tenants on their rights and help take action where landlords fail to meet their obligations.

What to do if the door frame is rotting?

A rotting door frame is more than a cosmetic issue, it can weaken the structure of your home, allow damp to spread, and create serious security concerns. If you are a tenant, it is the landlord’s responsibility to ensure that external doors and frames are kept in good repair. Once rot sets in, the problem will only worsen, so landlords are expected to act quickly when made aware of it.

If you notice signs of decay, such as soft wood, crumbling sections, gaps, or moisture damage, you should report the issue to your landlord in writing and keep clear evidence of the condition. If the landlord fails to carry out repairs within a reasonable timeframe, you may be able to pursue a housing disrepair claim, commonly referred to as rotten door compensation.

A successful claim can help ensure the necessary repairs are completed and provide compensation for the inconvenience, discomfort, or impact on your home caused by the disrepair.
