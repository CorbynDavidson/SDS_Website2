Compensation for Housing Disrepair Claims

The amount of compensation available in a  housing disrepair claim will depend on the circumstances of your case. Key factors include how serious the disrepair is, how long it has continued after your landlord was made aware of it, and how much of your home has been affected.

Where important rooms, services, or facilities have been unusable, such as a bathroom, kitchen, heating system, shower, or oven, compensation may be calculated by reference to the rent you paid during the period of disrepair. The greater the disruption to your use of the property, the higher the potential value of the claim may be.

As a general guide, housing disrepair compensation is often assessed at around 25% to 50% of the rent paid for the affected period. In more severe or long-running cases, compensation may exceed this range.

The Impact of Housing Disrepair on Waltham Forest Residents

Living with housing disrepair can affect your health, your finances, and your sense of security at home. What authorities govern housing disrepair in Waltham Forest?

Social housing in England can be handled by either a Council, which is a government authority, or by private Housing Associations which rent out properties. In Waltham Forest, your local Council is likely to be Waltham Forest Borough Council however other authorities may operate in the surrounding areas.

If your property is under the responsibility of a private Housing Association, this organisation must be properly registered and approved by the UK Government to operate in England. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get evicted while making a claim?

No, there are laws in place protecting Waltham Council and Housing Association tenants from being evicted for starting a housing disrepair compensation claim against their landlord. 

Do I still need to pay rent if I’m making a claim?

Yes, you should continue regular rent payments throughout a Waltham disrepair claim unless you have come to an agreement with the landlord about reducing or withholding rent. Rent arrears could make you liable for eviction, even if you have reduced your rent payments by a reasonable amount while the issues is not resolved or during the repairs if these affect the function of the house or some of the rooms.
