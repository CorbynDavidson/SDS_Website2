Understanding Housing Disrepair in Cheltenham

Housing disrepair happens when landlords, including Cheltenham Borough Council or local housing associations, fail to keep your rented property in proper condition. They have a legal duty to maintain the structure of the home and ensure essential services and fixtures are working safely and reliably.

The Impact of Housing Disrepair on Cheltenham Residents

Unresolved disrepair can affect far more than the condition of a property, it can undermine health, wellbeing, and everyday comfort. In Cheltenham, tenants in older housing can be particularly vulnerable to issues such as damp and mould, which often worsen in colder periods when ventilation is reduced. These conditions can aggravate asthma and other respiratory problems, and may be especially concerning for children, older residents, and anyone with existing medical needs.

Some defects also present immediate safety risks. Problems such as leaking roofs, broken windows, unstable plasterwork, or unsafe electrics can make parts of the home hazardous and are often clear signs that a landlord has not met their repair responsibilities.

The consequences are frequently financial as well as physical. Tenants may have to replace damaged belongings, deal with higher heating costs where systems are unreliable or inefficient, or spend money on short-term measures just to keep the home manageable. Many people also experience significant stress, loss of sleep, and ongoing anxiety when problems persist and complaints go unanswered.

Tenants do not have to accept these conditions. If a landlord has failed to take reasonable action, speaking to SDS Solicitors’ Cheltenham Housing Disrepair Solicitors can be the first step towards securing repairs and restoring the home to a safe, healthy standard.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get evicted while making a claim in Cheltenham?

No, there are laws in place protecting Cheltenham Council and Housing Association tenants from being evicted for starting a housing disrepair compensation claim against their landlord. 

Do I still need to pay rent if I’m making a claim?

Yes, you should continue regular rent payments throughout a Cheltenham disrepair claim unless you have come to an agreement with the landlord about reducing or withholding rent. Rent arrears could make you liable for eviction, even if you have reduced your rent payments by a reasonable amount while the issues is not resolved or during the repairs if these affect the function of the house or some of the rooms.
