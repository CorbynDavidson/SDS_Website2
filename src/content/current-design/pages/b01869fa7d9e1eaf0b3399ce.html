Understanding Housing Disrepair in Wigan

Housing disrepair happens when landlords, including Wigan Metropolitan Borough Council or local housing associations, fail to keep your rented property in proper condition. They have a legal duty to maintain the structure of the home and ensure essential services and fixtures are working safely and reliably.

Compensation for Housing Disrepair Claims

If your Wigan home has been affected by serious disrepair, you may be able to claim financial compensation and require your landlord to complete the necessary repairs. A housing disrepair claim is designed to recognise that you have been paying rent for a property that has not been maintained to the standard your landlord is legally required to provide.

The amount of compensation available will depend on the details of your case. This includes the nature of the disrepair, how severe the problem is, how long it has continued after your landlord was notified, and how much of the property has been affected. If important areas of the home have been difficult or impossible to use, this can also increase the value of the claim.

In many Wigan Housing Disrepair Claims, compensation is calculated as a percentage of the rent paid during the period when the property was affected. As a general guide, this often falls between 25% and 50% of the rent for that period, depending on the seriousness and duration of the disrepair. In more severe cases, a higher amount may be possible.

The Impact of Housing Disrepair on Wigan Residents

Living in a home affected by disrepair can have a serious impact on your health, comfort, and sense of security. For tenants in Wigan, damp and mould can develop where there are leaks, poor airflow, defective windows, or inadequate heating. These conditions can make the property feel cold and unhealthy, and may contribute to breathing problems, allergies, chest infections, or worsening symptoms for anyone with asthma or other medical conditions.

Other repair issues can make the home unsafe. Problems such as leaking roofs, cracked walls, damaged ceilings, broken windows, unsafe electrics, or faulty heating can affect how you use your property and may place you and your family at risk. When a landlord fails to deal with these hazards after being notified, it can leave tenants living in conditions that should not be accepted.

Disrepair can also create financial pressure. Belongings may be damaged by damp, leaks, or mould. Heating bills may rise if the property is difficult to keep warm. Some tenants also feel forced to spend their own money on short-term fixes while waiting for their landlord to respond.

The emotional effect can be just as difficult. Living in a property that feels neglected can cause stress, frustration, poor sleep, and anxiety about your home getting worse. If your landlord has failed to take proper action, speaking to our Wigan Housing Disrepair Solicitors can help you understand your rights and take the first step towards securing a safer, healthier home.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get evicted while making a claim?

No, there are laws in place protecting Wigan Council and Housing Association tenants from being evicted for starting a housing disrepair compensation claim against their landlord. 

Do I still need to pay rent if I’m making a claim?

Yes, you should continue regular rent payments throughout a Wigan disrepair claim unless you have come to an agreement with the landlord about reducing or withholding rent. Rent arrears could make you liable for eviction, even if you have reduced your rent payments by a reasonable amount while the issues is not resolved or during the repairs if these affect the function of the house or some of the rooms.
