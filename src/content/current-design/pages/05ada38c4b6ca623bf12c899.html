Understanding Housing Disrepair in Durham

Housing disrepair happens when landlords, including Durham County Council or local housing associations, fail to keep your rented property in proper condition. They have a legal duty to maintain the structure of the home and ensure essential services and fixtures are working safely and reliably.

The Impact of Housing Disrepair on Durham Residents

In Durham, ongoing disrepair can affect much more than the look of a property, it can disrupt daily life and have lasting consequences for health and wellbeing. Older homes can be more susceptible to damp and mould, particularly during colder periods when properties are harder to heat and ventilate. These conditions may worsen asthma and other respiratory problems, contribute to chest infections, and be especially concerning for children, older residents and anyone with existing medical conditions.

Some defects also create immediate safety concerns. Problems such as water ingress, broken windows, damaged plasterwork or unsafe electrics can make parts of the home hazardous and may indicate that a landlord has not met their repair obligations.

The impact is often financial as well. Tenants may face the cost of replacing belongings damaged by damp, increased energy bills when heating systems are inefficient or unreliable, and the expense of short-term measures while waiting for repairs. Many people also experience emotional strain, including stress, disrupted sleep and a reduced sense of comfort at home.

Where a landlord is not taking reasonable action, support is available. Taking advice from SDS Solicitors’ Durham Housing Disrepair Solicitors can help you understand your options for securing repairs and pursuing a fair outcome.

What authorities govern housing disrepair in Durham?

In Durham, social housing may be managed either by your local council or by a registered housing association. Most tenants will be under the responsibility of Durham County Council, although other councils within the North East area may also provide housing support or manage properties in surrounding districts.

If your property is under the responsibility of a private Housing Association, this organisation must be properly registered and approved by the UK Government to operate in England. You can check the details of your association on the list of registered providers on the Government’s website. In Durham and North East, some of the most common housing associations include:

 

  • Anchor Hanover
  • Hanover Housing Association
  • Human Kind
  • Bernicia

 

Other private providers operate in Durham, and you can still make a claim for disrepair issues if you are dealing with a provider who does not have a local office in Durham.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get evicted while making a claim in Durham?

No, there are laws in place protecting Durham Council and Housing Association tenants from being evicted for starting a housing disrepair compensation claim against their landlord. 

Do I still need to pay rent if I’m making a claim?

Yes, you should continue regular rent payments throughout a Durham disrepair claim unless you have come to an agreement with the landlord about reducing or withholding rent. Rent arrears could make you liable for eviction, even if you have reduced your rent payments by a reasonable amount while the issues is not resolved or during the repairs if these affect the function of the house or some of the rooms.
