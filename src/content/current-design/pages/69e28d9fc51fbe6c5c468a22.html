Understanding Housing Disrepair in Norwich

Housing disrepair happens when a landlord doesn’t carry out the repairs they’re legally responsible for, leaving tenants to live in unsafe or unfit conditions. In Norwich, this responsibility generally lies with Norwich City Council or a registered housing association. Compensation for Housing Disrepair Claims

If your landlord has neglected necessary repairs and your home has been affected, you may be entitled to compensation. The amount you could receive depends on how long the problem has lasted and how badly it has impacted your use of the property.

Compensation is typically calculated as a percentage of your rent for the period you were affected, usually ranging from 25% to 50%. In more severe or long-standing cases, that figure can be even higher.

What authorities govern housing disrepair in Norwich?

Tenants living in social housing across Norwich will usually rent through either the local council or a government-registered housing association. If you rent from a housing association, they also have a legal obligation to keep your home in a safe and suitable condition.

Am I eligible for a Housing Disrepair Claim in Norwich?

If you live, or previously lived, in a council or housing association property in Norwich and reported repair issues that were ignored, you may be eligible to make a claim. You don’t need to be currently living in the property to bring a claim, as long as the disrepair happened during your tenancy and caused loss, damage, or inconvenience.

You’ll usually need to show that:

  • You notified the landlord about the disrepair for over 6 months with no effort made on their part to fix the issues
  • It is a permanent property in Norwich
  • The disrepair was within the responsibility of a social landlord
  • You have not made a separate Housing Disrepair Claim in Norwich in the last 12 months
  • The health impacts or financial losses caused by the disrepair

It is vital to your claim to collect and keep evidence, such as photos and videos, demonstrating the disrepair and the problems it has caused you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get evicted while making a claim?

No, there are laws in place protecting Council and Housing Association tenants from being evicted for starting a housing disrepair compensation claim against their landlord. 

Do I still need to pay rent if I’m making a claim?

Yes, you should continue regular rent payments throughout a disrepair claim unless you have come to an agreement with the landlord about reducing or withholding rent. Rent arrears could make you liable for eviction, even if you have reduced your rent payments by a reasonable amount while the issues is not resolved or during the repairs if these affect the function of the house or some of the rooms.

What is the cut off for making a housing disrepair claim?

You have 6 years from when you first had the right to take action (6 months from when you first informed your landlord) for disrepair to make a claim.
