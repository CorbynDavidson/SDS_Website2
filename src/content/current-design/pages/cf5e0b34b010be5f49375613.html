Compensation for Housing Disrepair Claims

Compensation in a housing disrepair claim is based on the impact the problems have had on the home and the people living there. The value will depend on how serious the defects are, how long they have continued, and how far they have disrupted normal day-to-day living. Where important rooms or facilities have been affected, such as a bathroom that cannot be used properly, a kitchen that is difficult to cook in, or heating and hot water that have repeatedly failed, this loss of amenity can form a key part of the claim.

Compensation is often assessed by looking at the rent paid during the period the disrepair affected the property and considering the degree of disruption. The more severe the issues and the longer they have gone on, the stronger the claim is likely to be. Every case turns on its own facts, and the outcome will depend on the evidence available and the extent of the disrepair.

The Impact of Housing Disrepair on Bootle Residents

When disrepair is left unresolved, it can affect far more than the condition of a property, it can influence health, comfort and a household’s ability to feel settled at home. In Bootle, tenants often raise concerns about damp and mould, which can become persistent in older homes and may worsen during colder, wetter periods. Over time, these conditions can aggravate breathing problems and allergies, and can be particularly difficult for children, older residents, and anyone with asthma or other existing health conditions.

Other defects can create wider safety risks. Leaks, crumbling plaster, cracked walls, broken windows and unsafe electrics are issues that should be addressed promptly. When they are not, tenants may be left living around hazards and dealing with ongoing disruption, especially if repairs are repeatedly promised but not completed.

There can also be a real financial impact. Tenants may face damage to furniture, clothing or flooring, spend money on temporary fixes, or see energy bills rise where heating is unreliable or the home is hard to keep warm. The strain of being ignored can take an emotional toll too, affecting sleep, confidence and mental wellbeing, and leaving people reluctant to invite family or friends into the property.

No tenant should feel they have to accept these conditions. If you have reported disrepair and your landlord has not taken proper action, SDS Solicitors can advise you on the next steps towards securing repairs and restoring a safe, healthy living environment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get evicted while making a claim in Bootle?

No, there are laws in place protecting Bootle Council and Housing Association tenants from being evicted for starting a housing disrepair compensation claim against their landlord. 

Do I still need to pay rent if I’m making a claim in Bootle?

Yes, you should continue regular rent payments throughout a Bootle disrepair claim unless you have come to an agreement with the landlord about reducing or withholding rent. Rent arrears could make you liable for eviction, even if you have reduced your rent payments by a reasonable amount while the issues is not resolved or during the repairs if these affect the function of the house or some of the rooms.
