Understanding Housing Disrepair in Chester

Housing disrepair happens when landlords, including Cheshire West and Chester Council (CWAC) or local housing associations, fail to keep your rented property in proper condition. They have a legal duty to maintain the structure of the home and ensure essential services and fixtures are working safely and reliably.

Compensation for Housing Disrepair Claims

Where disrepair has been allowed to persist, tenants may be able to claim compensation as well as requiring the landlord to complete the repairs. SDS Solicitors handles Chester Housing Disrepair Claims, focusing on securing both the remedial works needed at the property and a fair settlement to reflect the inconvenience and loss of enjoyment caused.

Compensation is often assessed with reference to rent over the period affected. This usually involves considering your rent for the months the issues have continued and applying an appropriate percentage to reflect how serious the defects were and how much they interfered with normal use of the home. In general, the more severe the disrepair and the longer it remains unresolved, the greater the value of the claim may be.

The Impact of Housing Disrepair on Chester Residents

Poor living conditions can have a lasting impact on health, comfort and day-to-day life. In Chester, tenants in older properties may be more likely to experience damp and mould, particularly when colder weather and reduced ventilation make the problem worse. Prolonged exposure can aggravate asthma and other respiratory conditions, contribute to chest infections, and be especially worrying for children, older residents and anyone already managing a health issue.

Some forms of disrepair also create clear safety risks. Defects such as water ingress, damaged windows, failing plasterwork or unsafe electrics can make parts of the home dangerous to use and may indicate a serious failure to keep the property properly maintained.

The consequences are often financial as well. Tenants can be left dealing with damaged belongings, higher energy costs where heating systems are inefficient or unreliable, and the expense of temporary measures while waiting for repairs. The stress of living in an unfit home can also affect mental wellbeing, with many people reporting anxiety, disrupted sleep and a loss of enjoyment in their own home.

Tenants do not have to put up with it. If a landlord has not taken reasonable action, speaking to SDS Solicitors’ Chester Housing Disrepair Solicitors can be the first step towards securing repairs and restoring the home to a safe, healthy standard.

What authorities govern housing disrepair in Chester?

In Chester, social housing may be managed either by your local council or by a registered housing association. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get evicted while making a claim in Chester?

No, there are laws in place protecting Chester Council and Housing Association tenants from being evicted for starting a housing disrepair compensation claim against their landlord. 

Do I still need to pay rent if I’m making a claim?

Yes, you should continue regular rent payments throughout a Chester disrepair claim unless you have come to an agreement with the landlord about reducing or withholding rent. Rent arrears could make you liable for eviction, even if you have reduced your rent payments by a reasonable amount while the issues is not resolved or during the repairs if these affect the function of the house or some of the rooms.
