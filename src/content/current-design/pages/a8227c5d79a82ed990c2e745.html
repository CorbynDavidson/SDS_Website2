Understanding Defective Brickwork in Housing Disrepair

Defective brickwork refers to any damage or deterioration in a building's exterior or interior brick structure.

This can include cracks, crumbling mortar, loose bricks, and bulging walls, often caused by factors like poor maintenance, weathering, or age.

Under the Landlord and Tenant Act 1985 (Section 11), landlords are required to:

  • Keep the structure and exterior of the property in repair, including walls, brickwork, and pointing.
  • Address defects that compromise the safety and habitability of the property.

Additionally, the Homes (Fitness for Human Habitation) Act 2018 mandates that landlords ensure their properties remain safe and suitable for habitation, including resolving brickwork issues that could endanger tenants or lead to further disrepair.

If your landlord has been notified of defective brickwork and fails to take action, you may be eligible to bring a housing disrepair claim.

What Counts as Defective Brickwork

Defective brickwork refers to any damage or deterioration to the brick structure of your home that compromises its safety, appearance, or ability to protect against the elements. Common examples include:

  • Cracks in walls: These can indicate structural instability, potentially leading to more severe issues if left untreated.
  • Crumbling mortar: Weak or eroding mortar affects the bond between bricks, reducing the wall's strength and stability.
  • Loose or damaged bricks: Bricks that shift or show signs of breakage can weaken the property’s structure and pose safety risks.
  • Bulging walls: This occurs when bricks or mortar are displaced, often due to water damage or structural stress, and can lead to collapse if not repaired.
  • Damp patches around brickwork: Moisture entering through defective brickwork can cause internal dampness, mould, and further damage.
  • Visible gaps in pointing: Missing or deteriorated pointing allows water and pests to penetrate the walls, exacerbating disrepair.

If you notice any of these structural disrepair issues in your rented property, it’s crucial to inform your landlord promptly. If they fail to act, SDS Solicitors can help you pursue a housing condition claim to ensure necessary repairs are made.​​​​

Impact of Defective Brickwork on Your Property and Well-being 

Defective brickwork can have a wide range of consequences, from structural risks to health and safety hazards:

  • Structural disrepair: Cracks and loose bricks can compromise the strength of your home, posing a risk of collapse or significant damage during adverse weather conditions.
  • Leaks and damp: Gaps in damaged brickwork can allow rainwater to penetrate the property, leading to damp, mould and deterioration of internal wall surfaces. Continued moisture can cause plaster to crack, soften or fall away, this may also amount to crumbling plaster housing disrepair that your landlord is responsible for addressing.
  • Pest infestations: Weak or crumbling brickwork may create entry points for rodents, birds, or insects.

These issues not only affect your home’s safety but can also have a profound impact on your health and daily life. At SDS Solicitors, we can help you hold your landlord accountable and get the repairs needed to make your home safe and secure.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the dangers of defective brickwork?

Defective brickwork can lead to serious risks, including structural collapse, water ingress, damp, mould, and pest infestations, all of which can harm your health and safety.

How do I notify my landlord about defective brickwork?

You should report the issue in writing, providing detailed descriptions, photos, or videos as evidence. Keep a record of all communications with your landlord.

Can I claim compensation for repairs I had to carry out myself?

Yes, if you have had to pay for repairs because your landlord failed to act, you may be able to claim compensation for these costs as part of your housing disrepair claim.
