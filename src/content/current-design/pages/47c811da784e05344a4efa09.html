Understanding Disrepair in Private Rented Homes

Private landlords are legally responsible for making sure the homes they rent out are safe and fit to live in. This includes keeping the structure of the property in good condition and ensuring that key services are working properly.

If you’ve reported a repair issue to your landlord and they haven’t fixed it within a reasonable timeframe, they may be in breach of their legal duties under the Landlord and Tenant Act 1985 and the Homes (Fitness for Human Habitation) Act 2018.

These laws require landlords to keep the structure and exterior of the property in good repair, ensure that key installations are safe and in working order, and make sure the home is free from serious hazards that could affect your health or safety.

If your landlord has failed to meet these obligations and you’ve been left living in unsafe or unsuitable conditions, you may have the right to make a housing disrepair claim. Common disrepair issues include:

  • Damp and Mould
  • Flooding and Water Damage
  • Active Leaks and Plumbing
  • Defective Roofing and Gutters
  • Defective Boilers and Heating
  • Defective Windows and Doors
  • Defective Brickwork
  • Defective Spray Foam Insulation
  • Defective Wiring
  • Electrical Faults
  • Unsafe Flooring
  • Insect Infestations
  • Pest and Vermin Infestations

What Makes Claims Against Private Landlords Different?

Private landlords can vary a great deal in how they manage their properties. Many take their responsibilities seriously and act quickly when problems arise. But unfortunately, this isn’t always the case.

Some landlords delay repairs, ignore repeated complaints or avoid taking responsibility altogether. Unlike housing associations or councils, private landlords often don’t have a formal complaints process or a dedicated repair team. As a result, tenants are left chasing updates, dealing with poor-quality fixes and sometimes made to feel like they’re causing trouble just for speaking up.

In some cases, tenants are blamed for the damage or made to worry that reporting a problem will lead to rent increases or even eviction.

It’s important to know that your landlord is still bound by the same legal standards as any other housing provider. Under the law, they must keep your home safe and free from serious hazards. If they’ve failed to act after being notified of a problem, you may be entitled to take legal action and claim compensation.

The Impact of Disrepair on Private Tenants

Living with ongoing disrepair doesn’t just affect your home, it can have a serious impact on your health, wellbeing, and day-to-day life.

Damp and mould can lead to breathing problems and worsen existing health conditions. Faulty electrics or broken heating can make your home unsafe, particularly during winter or for young children and older adults. Leaks, infestations, and structural issues can damage your belongings and leave you out of pocket.

For many private tenants, the stress of living with these issues is made worse by the feeling that no one is listening. It can be exhausting to chase updates, especially if you’re working, raising a family, or dealing with health concerns. Some tenants are even made to feel that complaining could put their tenancy at risk.

The Fear of Eviction After Reporting Housing Disrepair

Many private tenants hesitate to take further steps when their landlord ignores repair requests, not because the disrepair isn’t serious but because they’re afraid of losing their home.

Some private landlords do attempt to end a tenancy rather than carry out the repairs they are legally responsible for. This is sometimes called a retaliatory eviction. While not all landlords behave this way, it’s a genuine concern and one we take seriously. You may be protected from eviction if:

  • Your tenancy started on or after 1 October 2015
  • You reported the issue in writing (such as by email or text)
  • You then contacted your local council because the landlord failed to act
  • The council issued an improvement notice or emergency works notice

Even if this specific protection doesn’t apply, it doesn’t mean you’re without options. It is unlawful for a landlord to evict you simply for exercising your legal right to live in a safe and habitable property.

Compensation for Private Landlord Housing Disrepair Claims

The amount you can claim depends on several factors, including how severe the disrepair is, how long it has been left unresolved, and the impact it has had on your life. This includes both the physical damage to your home and belongings, and the emotional stress or inconvenience you’ve experienced as a result. Compensation is typically awarded for:

  • The length of time you’ve lived with the disrepair
  • The seriousness of the issue and how it affected your day-to-day life
  • Any damage to your personal items, such as clothes, furniture, or appliances
  • Extra costs you’ve had to cover, like alternative heating, repairs, or even temporary accommodation

In most cases, tenants can expect to receive between 25% and 50% of their annual rent in compensation, depending on the circumstances.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I start a disrepair claim against a private landlord?

To begin a disrepair claim against a private landlord, you need to show that you reported the issue and gave your landlord time to respond. If the problem hasn’t been fixed, you may be entitled to compensation. Our team at SDS Solicitors will assess your case for free and guide you through every step of the process.

What evidence do I need for a disrepair claim against a private landlord?

For a successful disrepair claim against a private landlord, it’s important to show that your landlord knew about the problems and failed to fix them. This means keeping a clear record of every time you reported the issue. Ideally, you should report defects in writing, such as email, text message, or letter and keep copies of everything. You should also gather:

  • Photographs of the damage or defects
  • Records of phone calls or messages (including dates and what was said)
  • Reports from the council, environmental health, or independent surveyors
  • Evidence of any health impact, such as GP letters
  • Photos or receipts for any belongings damaged due to the disrepair

We recommend continuing to report the problem regularly, even if you feel ignored it helps show the issue was ongoing and not resolved. If you’ve been reporting problems through a letting agent, they have a duty to pass that information to your landlord.

The more organised your evidence, the stronger your private housing disrepair claim will be and we can help you pull it all together.

What if my landlord says the disrepair is my fault?

It’s not uncommon for landlords to try and shift blame onto the tenant to avoid responsibility. They may claim the damage was caused by neglect or misuse of the property. But just because they say it’s your fault, doesn’t mean it is and they have to prove it.

In a housing disrepair claim against a private landlord, the burden of proof is on the landlord. We help you build a strong case using clear evidence such as photographs, dated text messages, emails, inspection reports, or environmental health notices. If you’ve been reporting the issue regularly, and especially if other tenants or professionals have noted the same problems, this can be powerful proof that the disrepair wasn’t caused by you.

This tactic is often used to intimidate or discourage tenants from taking action. But at SDS Solicitors, we know the law and we’ll protect you from being unfairly blamed. If your private landlord disrepair claim is valid, we’ll help you prove it and hold your landlord accountable.

Do I need to speak to my landlord again before making a claim?

No. If you’ve already reported the issue and your landlord hasn’t acted within a reasonable time, you are not required to keep chasing them. In many housing disrepair claims against private landlords, tenants have already made multiple attempts to get repairs done before coming to us and that’s enough.
