Understanding Housing Disrepair in Hartlepool

Housing disrepair refers to a failure by landlords, whether Hartlepool Borough Council or housing association, to keep a rented property in good repair. Compensation for Housing Disrepair Claims

Compensation in housing disrepair claims is not a fixed figure, it will depend on how serious the defects are, how long they have been left unresolved, and the extent to which they have affected daily living. Where parts of the home or essential facilities have not been usable for a period of time, tenants may be able to recover compensation calculated by reference to their rent, reflecting the loss of use and the disruption caused.

As a general guide, awards are often assessed at around 25% to 50% of the annual rent for the relevant period, although particularly severe situations can justify a higher level of compensation. SDS Solicitors will review the claim carefully to ensure all eligible losses are included, taking into account matters such as financial outlay, damage to belongings, inconvenience, and any impact on health and wellbeing.

The Impact of Housing Disrepair on Hartlepool Residents

Ongoing disrepair can affect every aspect of home life, placing pressure on physical health, emotional wellbeing and household finances. In Hartlepool, tenants often report problems such as damp and mould, particularly in older properties or homes where ventilation and insulation are poor. When these conditions are left unresolved, they can contribute to breathing difficulties, allergies and longer-term health concerns, especially for children, older residents and anyone with existing medical issues. Where disrepair also involves structural defects, the situation can become even more serious, leaving parts of the home unsafe or difficult to use properly.

The knock-on impact is often financial as well. Many tenants face damage to belongings, higher energy bills where heating systems are inefficient, and the cost of temporary measures when landlords do not step in. At the same time, living in an unfit home can be emotionally draining, with residents commonly experiencing stress, disturbed sleep, embarrassment and a reduced ability to feel comfortable in their own space.

Tenants do not have to accept these conditions indefinitely. If a landlord is not addressing the problems, seeking early legal advice can be the first step towards securing the repairs needed and improving living conditions.

What authorities govern housing disrepair in Hartlepool?

Social housing in England can be handled by either a Council, which is a government authority, or by private Housing Associations which rent out properties. In Hartlepool, and in the wider County Durham area, your local Council is likely to be Hartlepool Borough Council however other authorities may operate in the surrounding areas.

 

  • Tees Valley Combined Authority
  • Wynyard Parish Council
  • Headland Parish Council

 

If your property is under the responsibility of a private Housing Association, this organisation must be properly registered and approved by the UK Government to operate in England. You can check the details of your association on the list of registered providers on the Government’s website. In Hartlepool and County Durham, some of the most common housing associations include:

 

  • Community Campus 87

  • Guinness Trust Group
  • Hartlepool NDC Trust

 

Other private providers operate in Hartlepool, and you can still make a claim for disrepair issues if you are dealing with a provider who does not have a local office in Hartlepool.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get evicted while making a claim?

No, there are laws in place protecting Hartlepool Council and Housing Association tenants from being evicted for starting a housing disrepair compensation claim against their landlord. 

Do I still need to pay rent if I’m making a claim?

Yes, you should continue regular rent payments throughout a Hartlepool disrepair claim unless you have come to an agreement with the landlord about reducing or withholding rent. Rent arrears could make you liable for eviction, even if you have reduced your rent payments by a reasonable amount while the issues is not resolved or during the repairs if these affect the function of the house or some of the rooms.
