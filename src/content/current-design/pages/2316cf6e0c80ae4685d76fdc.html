Housing Disrepair Claim for Broken Electrics

Electrical faults in your home can be disruptive, inconvenient, and potentially dangerous. From malfunctioning appliances to faulty sockets or repeated power outages, problems with household electrics can significantly impact your quality of life and even pose serious safety risks.

As a tenant in social housing, your landlord is legally obligated to provide a safe and functional home. This includes ensuring all electrical installations, fixtures, and appliances are properly maintained. If your landlord has failed to address electrical faults in your property, you may be entitled to bring a housing disrepair claim for repairs and compensation.

Understanding Electrical Faults in Housing Disrepair

Electrical faults refer to issues affecting the functionality, safety, or reliability of the electrical systems, fixtures, and appliances within your home. These problems can arise from general wear and tear, inadequate maintenance, or improper installations.

Unlike Dangerous Electrical Wiring Disrepair, which relates specifically to problems with the electrical infrastructure of the property, electrical faults cover a wider range of issues, from faulty sockets to malfunctioning landlord-provided appliances.

Your landlord has a legal responsibility to ensure that all electrical systems and appliances in your home are safe and fully operational. Under the Electrical Safety Standards in the Private Rented Sector (England) Regulations 2020, landlords are required to have electrical installations inspected by a qualified electrician at least once every five years.

Additionally, the Landlord and Tenant Act 1985 mandates that all rented properties are maintained in good repair, including resolving electrical faults promptly when reported.

If your landlord fails to address electrical faults in your home after being notified, it not only creates unsafe living conditions but also breaches their legal obligations.

In such cases, you may be entitled to bring a housing disrepair claim to secure repairs and compensation.

Common Types of Electrical Faults

Electrical faults can take many forms, impacting your home in a variety of ways. Some of the most common examples include:

  • Faulty Sockets: Power outlets that no longer function properly or pose risks such as sparking or overheating.
  • Malfunctioning Light Fixtures: Lights that flicker, fail to turn on, or operate inconsistently.
  • Power Failures: Intermittent or prolonged power outages affecting part or all of your home.
  • Defective Appliances: Landlord-provided appliances, such as ovens or refrigerators, that fail to work safely or efficiently.
  • Faulty Electrical Installations: Issues with fuse boxes, circuit breakers, or other essential components that cause frequent tripping or power instability.

These issues range from inconvenient to outright hazardous, depending on the severity. If you experience any of these problems, it’s vital to report them to your landlord promptly and ensure they are addressed by a qualified professional.

The Impact of Electrical Faults on Your Home and Well-being

Electrical faults can have a significant impact on your ability to live safely and comfortably in your home. They may cause interruptions to essential daily tasks, such as cooking, heating, and lighting, leading to inconvenience and frustration.

In more severe cases, electrical faults can be hazardous. Faulty sockets or overloaded circuits can spark fires, while malfunctioning appliances or exposed electrics may cause electric shocks. Even intermittent issues like power cuts or flickering lights can create stress and anxiety for tenants, particularly if the problems persist over time.

Compensation for Electrical Faults in Housing Disrepair

If your landlord neglects their responsibility to resolve electrical faults, you may be entitled to compensation. This can depend on:

  • The severity and duration of the problem.
  • The impact on your daily life, such as the inconvenience caused by lack of power.
  • Any financial losses incurred, such as costs to replace damaged items or temporary alternative accommodation.

Compensation for electrical faults is typically calculated as a percentage of your annual rent, ranging from 25% to 50% in severe cases. Our dedicated housing disrepair solicitors will work with you to ensure you receive a fair settlement while securing the necessary repairs to your home.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between electrical faults and defective wiring?

Electrical faults cover broader issues, including problems with sockets, appliances, or fixtures. Defective wiring specifically relates to the property’s wiring system.

Can I claim if a landlord-provided appliance is faulty?

Yes, landlords are responsible for maintaining appliances they provide. If these are unsafe or stop working, you may be able to include this in your claim.

How quickly must my landlord address electrical faults?

Landlords are expected to resolve electrical faults promptly, especially those that pose safety risks or disrupt essential living conditions.
