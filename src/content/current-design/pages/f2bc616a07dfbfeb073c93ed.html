Gutters & Roofing Disrepair Compensation

Defective roofing and guttering can cause severe damage to your home and pose significant risks to your safety and well-being. Water ingress and leaks caused by defective roofs or guttering can lead to Flooding & Water Damage Disrepair, as well as damp, mould and wider structural problems if left unresolved.

If you are a tenant in social housing, such as a council house or flat, or a property managed by a housing association or charitable organisation, it’s your landlord's responsibility to ensure that your home remains safe and in good condition.

If they have failed to repair issues with your roofing or guttering after being informed, you may be entitled to compensation through a housing disrepair claim.

The Impact of Defective Roofing and Guttering

Issues with the roof and gutters can cause serious problems in your home, affecting both your living conditions and your well-being.

When the roof or gutters are damaged or blocked, water cannot drain properly, leading to leaks, damp patches, and mould growth. Damp conditions create an ideal environment for mould, which can spread quickly across walls and ceilings and may give tenants grounds to pursue a Damp & Mould Claim where the underlying defect has not been repaired This can be particularly harmful to those with asthma, respiratory conditions, or weakened immune systems, as exposure to mould spores can trigger breathing difficulties, persistent coughing, and skin irritation.

Water damage from leaks can also weaken the structure of your home. If left unresolved, it can cause plaster to deteriorate or crumble, potentially leading to Crumbling Plaster Housing Disrepair, as well as sagging ceilings and rotting wooden supports. In severe cases, long-term exposure to water can lead to costly repairs and make parts of your home unsafe. Additionally, leaks and dampness can damage furniture, carpets, and personal belongings, creating further disruption and expense.

If your landlord has failed to fix these issues despite being informed, you may have the right to make a housing disrepair claim.

Landlord Responsibilities

According to the Landlord and Tenant Act 1985, Section 11, your landlord is legally required to maintain the structure and exterior of the property, including roofs, gutters, and drainage systems​. This obligation applies whether you are renting from a council, housing association, or a private landlord. The law ensures that the property remains fit for habitation, and your landlord must carry out necessary repairs within a reasonable timeframe once they have been notified of the issue.

If your landlord has ignored your requests or delayed repairs, you are entitled to take legal action. At SDS Solicitors, we can help you navigate this process.

How to Make a Housing Disrepair Claim

To make a successful claim, you need to show that:

  1. You notified your landlord of the disrepair: Evidence that you have reported the roofing or guttering issue to your landlord is essential. Written communication such as emails or letters is preferable.
  2. The landlord failed to act within a reasonable time: If your landlord does not address the problem promptly, you may be able to claim compensation.
  3. You have suffered losses: This can include damage to your belongings, health problems, or the general loss of enjoyment of your home due to living in unsafe or uncomfortable conditions.

We can assist you in gathering the necessary evidence and building a strong case.

Compensation for Defective Roofing and Guttering Claims

The compensation you receive will depend on the severity of the damage and the impact it has had on your life. This could include:

  • Repair Costs: Ensuring that the necessary roofing or guttering repairs are carried out at no cost to you.
  • Health Damages: Compensation for any health issues caused by exposure to damp, mould, or leaks.
  • Financial Losses: Reimbursement for any property damage or additional living expenses incurred as a result of the disrepair.
