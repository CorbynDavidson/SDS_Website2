Compensation for Housing Disrepair Claims

Living in a property affected by disrepair is more than just an inconvenience. It can have a serious impact on your daily life. If your landlord has failed to deal with the issues properly, you may be entitled to compensation through a Dover Housing Disrepair Claim.

The Impact of Housing Disrepair on Dover Residents

In Dover, unresolved disrepair can place real pressure on households, affecting health, wellbeing and household budgets. Damp and mould are frequent issues in older or poorly ventilated homes and, when left untreated, can aggravate asthma and other respiratory conditions, contribute to allergies, and lead to ongoing illness. Where defects also include structural problems or safety-related faults, a property may become difficult, and sometimes unsafe, to live in.

The consequences are often felt financially as well. Tenants may face the cost of replacing belongings damaged by moisture, increased energy bills where heating systems are inefficient or unreliable, and the expense of temporary measures while waiting for repairs. Many people also experience emotional strain, including stress, embarrassment and a reduced sense of comfort in their own home.

Where a landlord is not putting things right, there are options available. Our housing disrepair solicitors can help identify what the landlord is responsible for, what evidence is needed, and how to take steps towards getting the repairs completed and the situation resolved.

What authorities govern housing disrepair in Dover?

Social housing in England can be handled by either a Council, which is a government authority, or by private Housing Associations which rent out properties. In Dover, and in the wider Kent area, your local Council is likely to be Dover District Council however other authorities may operate in the surrounding areas.

If your property is under the responsibility of a private Housing Association, this organisation must be properly registered and approved by the UK Government to operate in England.   • Sanctuary Housing Association

If you believe you have grounds for a housing disrepair claim for a Dover home, you must be able to answer the following questions:

  • Have you notified the landlord about the disrepair for over 6 months?
  • Is this a permanent property in Dover?
  • Is the disrepair within the responsibility of a social landlord?
  • Have you had a Housing Disrepair Claim in Dover in the last 12 months?
  • Have you taken photographs, videos, and notes detailing the problems?
  • Have you gathered up-to-date records of health impacts or financial losses caused by the disrepair?
  • Are you in rental arrears? If yes, do you have a payment plan?

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get evicted while making a claim in Dover?

No, there are laws in place protecting Dover Council and Housing Association tenants from being evicted for starting a housing disrepair compensation claim against their landlord. 

Do I still need to pay rent if I’m making a claim?

Yes, you should continue regular rent payments throughout a Dover disrepair claim unless you have come to an agreement with the landlord about reducing or withholding rent. Rent arrears could make you liable for eviction, even if you have reduced your rent payments by a reasonable amount while the issues is not resolved or during the repairs if these affect the function of the house or some of the rooms.
