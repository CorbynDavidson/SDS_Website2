Understanding Housing Disrepair in Blackburn

Housing disrepair occurs when a landlord, whether Blackburn with Darwen Borough Council or a housing association, fails to maintain a rented property to a safe and livable standard. This includes ensuring the structure remains in good condition and that essential systems such as heating, plumbing, and electrical wiring are properly maintained. When these responsibilities are neglected, tenants may be left in unsafe or unhealthy living conditions, with the right to take legal action to secure repairs. Compensation for Housing Disrepair Claims

The amount of compensation you can receive from housing disrepair claims depends on the severity of the issue, compensation is designed to reflect the real impact of living with problems that should have been put right. The value of a claim will depend on how serious the defects are, how long they have continued, and how much they have affected day-to-day life. Where essential facilities have been compromised, such as heating and hot water, bathing, cooking, or safe electrics, the loss of use and disruption caused can form a key part of the claim.

In practice, compensation is often assessed by considering the rent paid during the period the disrepair affected the property and the extent to which the home (or particular rooms) could not be properly used. The greater the disruption, the more significant the impact on the household is likely to be. Each case is different, and the outcome will always depend on the specific circumstances and evidence available.

The Impact of Housing Disrepair on Blackburn Residents

When disrepair is left unresolved, it can affect far more than the condition of a property, it can disrupt everyday life and place pressure on health, finances and wellbeing. In Blackburn, tenants often report problems such as damp and mould, leaks and cold spots, particularly in older properties where insulation and ventilation may be less effective. Over time, these conditions can aggravate asthma and other respiratory issues and may contribute to persistent coughing, wheezing or recurring chest infections, especially for children, older residents and anyone with existing health concerns.

Disrepair can also create safety risks. Cracked walls, failing plaster, water ingress, broken windows, unsafe electrics and unstable flooring are not issues tenants should be expected to live with. When repairs are delayed despite being reported, homes can feel unsettled and, in some cases, genuinely hazardous.

The practical and financial consequences can be significant as well. Tenants may face damage to furniture, clothing or flooring, increased energy costs where heating systems are unreliable or the home is difficult to keep warm, and the added expense of temporary fixes while waiting for action. The ongoing stress of living in poor conditions can also impact sleep and mental wellbeing, leaving people feeling worn down in the place where they should feel most secure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get evicted while making a claim in Blackburn?

No, there are laws in place protecting Blackburn Council and Housing Association tenants from being evicted for starting a housing disrepair compensation claim against their landlord. 

Do I still need to pay rent if I’m making a claim in Blackburn?

Yes, you should continue regular rent payments throughout a Blackburn disrepair claim unless you have come to an agreement with the landlord about reducing or withholding rent. Rent arrears could make you liable for eviction, even if you have reduced your rent payments by a reasonable amount while the issues is not resolved or during the repairs if these affect the function of the house or some of the rooms.
