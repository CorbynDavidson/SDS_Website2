Understanding London & Quadrant Housing Trust Disrepair Claims

Tenants of London & Quadrant Housing Trust are entitled to expect their homes to remain safe, secure, and suitable for occupation. Where important repairs are left outstanding, essential services fail, or defects are allowed to worsen, the condition of the property may amount to housing disrepair.

As a provider of Supported Housing, London & Quadrant Housing Trust must take its responsibilities seriously, particularly where residents may depend on reliable heating, safe access, and properly maintained communal facilities. Its obligations are supported by legislation including the Landlord and Tenant Act 1985 and the Homes (Fitness for Human Habitation) Act 2018, which require landlords to maintain key parts of rented homes and ensure properties remain fit to live in.

Problems that may give rise to a Housing Disrepair Claim can include:

  • Heating systems & defective boilers: Faults with boilers, communal heating systems, or temperature controls can leave residents without essential warmth or hot water.
  • Damp and mould: Water ingress, poor insulation, defective ventilation, or other faults within the building can allow damp and mould to develop and spread.
  • Defective safety or access features: Broken lifts, unreliable emergency call systems, damaged handrails, or poorly maintained shared areas can create additional risks for residents.
  • Leaks and plumbing: Leaking pipes, drainage failures, faulty toilets, or interruptions to the water supply can require urgent investigation and repair.
  • Pest and vermin Infestation: Structural gaps, damaged walls, or defects in communal areas can provide access points for rodents or insects and may require remedial building work.

Once London & Quadrant Housing Trust has been notified about a problem, it should take appropriate steps to investigate and arrange repairs within a reasonable period. The expected response time will depend on the nature of the defect and the level of risk it presents.

London & Quadrant Housing Trust aims to attend emergency repairs within 24 hours. If you have reported a serious defect and it has not been dealt with appropriately, you may be entitled to seek the necessary repairs and pursue compensation through a L&Q Housing Trust Disrepair Claim.

Who Regulates London & Quadrant Housing Trust And Where Are They Based?

Social housing in England is managed by either a local Council or by private Housing Associations. If your property is under the responsibility of a private provider like London & Quadrant Housing Trust, the organisation must be properly registered and approved by the UK Government to operate.

For tenants of London & Quadrant Housing Trust, the following details are essential for establishing legal accountability:

  • Head Office Address: 29–35 West Ham Lane, Stratford, London, E15 4PH
  • Regulatory Number: IP030441

The Regulator of Social Housing (RSH) oversees the performance of London & Quadrant Housing Trust at an organisational level, ensuring they are financially viable and meet consumer standards for safety and quality.

Individual complaints and disputes are handled by the Housing Ombudsman Service. They have the power to investigate "maladministration" and can order London & Quadrant Housing Trust to carry out repairs, apologise, and pay financial compensation. You are entitled to make a claim for disrepair even if you do not live near their head office.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get evicted while making a claim?

No, there are laws in place protecting Council and Housing Association tenants from being evicted for starting a housing disrepair compensation claim against their landlord. 

Do I still need to pay rent if I’m making a claim?

Yes, you should continue regular rent payments throughout a disrepair claim unless you have come to an agreement with the landlord about reducing or withholding rent. Rent arrears could make you liable for eviction, even if you have reduced your rent payments by a reasonable amount while the issues is not resolved or during the repairs if these affect the function of the house or some of the rooms.

What should I do if L&Q Housing Trust keeps delaying my repairs?

Keep a clear record of when you reported the problem, any reference numbers provided, photographs of the disrepair, and all correspondence with L&Q Housing Trust. If the issue remains unresolved after a reasonable period, you may be able to pursue a London & Quadrant Trust Housing Disrepair Claim.

Can I make a claim if the disrepair is in a communal area?

Potentially, yes. If the problem affects a communal area that L&Q Housing Trust is responsible for maintaining, such as a shared hallway, lift, entrance, or other common facility, you may still have grounds to take action if the defect has not been properly addressed.
