Compensation for Housing Disrepair Claims

Compensation for housing disrepair depends on the seriousness of the problem, how long it has continued, and how it has affected your daily life. If essential parts of your home such as your kitchen or bathroom have not been usable, you may be entitled to a percentage of your rent for the time those areas were out of use.

In many cases, tenants can expect to receive between 25% and 50% of their annual rent as compensation. In more severe situations, this amount may be higher. The Impact of Housing Disrepair on Tower Hamlets Residents

Living with unresolved disrepair can place a heavy strain on your health, finances, and day-to-day well-being. For tenants in Tower Hamlets, damp and mould can develop where there are leaks, poor ventilation, defective windows, cold surfaces, or heating problems. These conditions can make a home feel unhealthy and uncomfortable, and may worsen asthma, allergies, breathing difficulties, or other existing medical conditions.

Structural issues can also make the property unsafe. Problems such as cracked walls, damaged ceilings, roof leaks, unstable flooring, broken windows, or faulty electrics can affect how you use your home and may create risks for you and your family. If your landlord does not repair these defects, the condition of the property may continue to deteriorate.

The financial impact can be significant too. Damp, mould, and leaks may damage furniture, clothing, bedding, carpets, and other belongings. Poor heating or insulation can increase energy bills, while repeated delays and ignored complaints can cause stress, embarrassment, frustration, and a loss of enjoyment of your home.

If your landlord has been notified about the disrepair and has failed to take proper action, you do not have to keep living in these conditions. What authorities govern housing disrepair in Tower Hamlets?

Social housing in England can be handled by either a Council, which is a government authority, or by private Housing Associations which rent out properties. In Tower Hamlets, your local Council is likely to be Tower Hamlets Borough Council however other authorities may operate in the surrounding areas.

If your property is under the responsibility of a private Housing Association, this organisation must be properly registered and approved by the UK Government to operate in England. You can check the details of your association on the list of registered providers on the Government’s website. In Tower Hamlets, some of the most common housing associations include:

  • Genesis Housing Association
  • Gateway Housing Association
  • Spitalfields Housing Association
  • Providence Row Housing Association
  • Caplin House
  • East End Homes
  • Poplar Harca Association

Other private providers operate in Tower Hamlets, and you can still make a claim for disrepair issues if you are dealing with a provider who does not have a local office in Tower Hamlets.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get evicted while making a claim?

No, there are laws in place protecting Tower Hamlets Council and Housing Association tenants from being evicted for starting a housing disrepair compensation claim against their landlord. 

Do I still need to pay rent if I’m making a claim?

Yes, you should continue regular rent payments throughout a Tower Hamlets disrepair claim unless you have come to an agreement with the landlord about reducing or withholding rent. Rent arrears could make you liable for eviction, even if you have reduced your rent payments by a reasonable amount while the issues is not resolved or during the repairs if these affect the function of the house or some of the rooms.
